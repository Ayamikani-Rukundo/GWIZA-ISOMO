Build a polished, lightweight full-stack web application called **GWIZA Research** for the Gwiza Startup team.

## 1. PROJECT PURPOSE

This is a **research data-collection website**, not the final mental-health product.

Gwiza Startup is conducting action research on:

**Mental Health Support Gap → Secondary-School Students → Rwanda**

Our main research question is:

**“What is the mental-health support gap among secondary-school students in Rwanda, and what can be done to improve the support available to them?”**

The purpose of this website is to collect primary research data that helps us understand:

- students' experiences and challenges
- how mental-health challenges affect school life
- awareness of available support
- who students turn to when they need help
- barriers to seeking support
- what support currently exists
- what students feel is missing
- what kind of support students would actually use
- whether technology could realistically improve access
- what teachers, counsellors, school staff, and professionals observe

IMPORTANT:

Do NOT assume that an AI chatbot, mobile app, online psychologist service, peer-support platform, or any other technology is automatically the solution.

The research must help us discover the problem first.

The research flow is:

**PROBLEM → CURRENT SUPPORT → GAP → STUDENT NEEDS → POSSIBLE SOLUTIONS**

# 11. QUESTIONNAIRE DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Follow these principles carefully.

Questions must:

- be simple
- use natural language
- avoid academic jargon
- avoid leading respondents toward a particular answer
- avoid double-barrelled questions
- avoid unnecessary personal information
- provide “Prefer not to say” where appropriate
- allow “Other”
- use logical ordering
- combine closed questions with a small number of open questions

Avoid:

“Do you agree that schools need better mental-health technology?”

This is leading.

Instead ask:

“What do you think is missing from the support currently available?”

Then later ask about technology separately.

Use a mixture of:
- multiple choice
- checkboxes
- 1–5 ratings
- Likert-style questions
- short open-ended questions

The goal is **usable research data**, not a huge questionnaire.
